Project Overview
Criterion 2
What is TerpTaste? A location-based mobile app helping College Park students discover, track, and evaluate nearby restaurants. Users filter by cuisine, price, diet, and hours; see what friends are eating via a real check-in feed; and resolve the classic group deadlock through a live Group Vote feature.
🔍
Smart Discovery
Browse 22+ real College Park spots filtered by price, diet, distance & hours.
👥
Friends Feed
Real check-ins from people you know — no bots, no paid reviews.
🗳️
Group Vote
Add options, vote live, and settle the group chat in under 60 seconds.
🎲
Surprise Me
One tap picks a random restaurant — for when decision fatigue wins.
3
Problem Statement
Criterion 3
The problem: College Park has a dense, constantly-changing food scene. Students — especially freshmen and transfers — face overwhelming choice paralysis, distrust of generic app reviews, and no good tool for making group dining decisions. Groups regularly spend 45+ minutes in chat threads only to default to Chipotle.
🎯 User Pain Points
Too many options with no College Park context. Anonymous reviews feel fake or AI-generated. Coordinating with friends across different preferences is exhausting. Most students don't know half the spots within walking distance.
📍 Scope & Impact
Affects all ~40,000 UMD students, with freshest pain felt by ~8,000 new arrivals each year. The dining decision happens 1–3x daily — making it one of the most frequent micro-stresses in student life.
4
Research Questions
Criterion 4
Primary Research Question
"How do students in College Park currently decide where to eat, and what challenges do they face when trying to decide?"
Sub-question 1
What information do students most need when choosing a restaurant (price, hours, distance, peer reviews)?
Sub-question 2
How do group dynamics affect the dining decision, and what tools would most reduce group decision time?
5
SMART Goals
Criterion 5
S — Specific
Build a mobile-first app focused exclusively on food discovery for the College Park / UMD area, covering at least 20 real local restaurants with cuisine type, price, hours, and dietary information.
M — Measurable
Track at least 22 restaurants. Achieve a task success rate of ≥70% across 4 core usability tasks in testing. Survey satisfaction with existing tools (Google Maps, Yelp) as a baseline.
A — Achievable
Use publicly available restaurant data and a single HTML/CSS/JS prototype deployable via GitHub Pages — within scope for a 5-person student team over one semester.
R — Relevant
Directly addresses decision fatigue, distrust of anonymous reviews, and group coordination — the three highest-pain issues surfaced in our interviews and surveys.
T — Time-bound
Research completed by Week 8. Prototype live by Week 12. Usability testing conducted by Week 14. Final showcase-ready by end of semester.
6
Research Overview
Criterion 6
We used three complementary methods to understand not just what students do, but why — and how widespread those frustrations truly are. Each method captured different dimensions that the others couldn't.
🎙️ Semi-Structured Interviews
Captures the "why" behind behavior. Two-part format: general dining habits, then a specific frustrating recent experience. 5 participants.
👁️ Contextual Observation
AEIOU framework at STAMP, Baltimore Ave, and campus dining halls. Revealed real-time decision patterns interviews can't capture.
📊 Survey
Likert scales, ranked feature preferences, and open-ended questions distributed via Reddit, GroupMe, Discord, and Yik Yak. Validated interview patterns at scale.

8
Contextual Observation
Criterion 8
Using the AEIOU framework, we observed students at STAMP Student Union, Baltimore Ave shopping corridor, and near campus dining halls. The core insight: people don't always behave how they describe in interviews.
📍 Locations
STAMP Student Union food court, Baltimore Ave restaurant corridor, McKeldin Library entrance, and the Route 1 dining strip between classes.
🔑 Key Findings
Students physically walked 3–5 restaurants before deciding. Most had Google Maps or Yelp open but these apps were not helping them choose faster.
📱 Observed Behaviors
Price-checking menus in doorways. Texting friends for input mid-walk. Giving up and returning to familiar chains despite wanting something new.

13
Prototype
Criterion 13
TerpTaste — Live Prototype The app you're using right now is the prototype. Built as a single-file HTML/CSS/JS application, deployed via GitHub Pages.
Open Prototype ↗
Design Rationale
Every feature maps directly to a research finding. The Friends Feed addresses distrust of anonymous reviews. Group Vote eliminates 45-minute group chats. Filters address price/diet/hours as the top three decision factors. "Surprise Me" handles decision fatigue directly.
Technical Stack
Vanilla HTML, CSS custom properties, and JavaScript — no build tools required. 22 real College Park restaurants hardcoded with accurate data. Hosted on GitHub Pages at dabarjel.github.io/TerpTaste.
Key Screens
🏠 Home / Discover — filtered restaurant cards with peer quotes
🔍 Filter Panel — price, cuisine, diet, hours, distance
📄 Restaurant Detail — menu, hours, dietary info, save & check-in
🗳️ Group Vote — live voting with real-time results
👥 Friends Feed — peer check-ins and recommendations
❤️ Saved Spots — personal shortlist
AI Tools Disclosure: Claude (Anthropic) was used to assist with code generation, content structuring, and UI refinement during prototype development. All restaurant data, research content, and design decisions were produced by the team.

5 UMD students matching our target population: on-campus, off-campus, no meal plan, dietary restrictions, and new student (freshman/transfer). Same participants as initial interviews for consistency.

Final Design Outcomes
We shipped a fully functional prototype covering every feature surfaced in research — smart filtering, peer-trusted reviews via the Friends Feed, and a Group Vote that replaces the 45-minute group chat. 22 real restaurants with accurate data. 100% task completion in testing.
💡
Key Takeaways
The triangulation of interviews + observation + surveys was essential — interview self-reporting alone would have missed the physical decision-fatigue behaviors we observed in the field. The "social trust" insight (71% prefer peer reviews) became the core differentiator of our design.
📚
Learning Experience
Research-first design genuinely changes what you build. Early assumptions (that a recommendation algorithm was the main feature) were overturned by user data in favor of social trust and group tools. Iterating quickly with a lightweight HTML prototype allowed us to test real flows rather than static mockups.
🚀
Future Direction
Live API integration with Google Places for real-time hours and photos. Verified UMD login (via .edu email) to power authentic Friends Feed data. Personalization engine that learns preferences over time. Native iOS/Android app. Partnership with local restaurants for accurate wait time data.
Unanswered Questions: How would preferences shift across time of day (lunch vs. late-night)? Which social features drive the most repeat engagement? Would a personalized recommendation engine outperform the filter system for discovery? These become the next research cycle.
